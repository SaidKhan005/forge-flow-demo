Responsible service is about ensuring that alcohol is served safely and legally, creating a safe and enjoyable environment for everyone. Using a range of critical practices including over-serving prevention by understanding the limits of patrons, checking IDs diligently to verify age, and recognizing signs of intoxication early on. These measures not only safeguard the well-being of guests but also create a culture of safety and responsibility that resonates throughout the entire establishment.

Both employers and employees share the responsibility of ensuring that alcohol is served in a manner that prioritizes safety and legality. Staff should be well-trained to make informed decisions - whether it's deciding when to cut someone off or understanding the legal liabilities tied to alcohol service. This knowledge helps maintain compliance with local laws and enhances the professional image of the establishment. When everyone on the team is equipped with the knowledge and skills to make responsible decisions, the entire establishment benefits from a reputation built on care and diligence.

## Governing Bodies

In Newfoundland and Labrador, the sale, importation, and distribution of alcohol are governed by a robust legal framework that ensures responsible management and oversight. At the heart of this framework are two pivotal pieces of legislation: The Newfoundland and Labrador Control Act and the Liquor Corporation Act. Together, these laws lay the groundwork for the governance of alcohol-related activities in the province, ensuring that they are conducted safely, ethically, and in accordance with the values of the community.

The Liquor Control Act serves as the primary legislation for regulating liquor sales, providing a comprehensive set of rules regarding licenses, permits, pricing, and operational standards. This act empowers the Newfoundland and Labrador Liquor Corporation (NLC), the Crown's agent, to oversee the importation, sales, and distribution of alcohol across the province. By establishing a clear framework for issuing, refusing, or suspending liquor licenses and permits, the Liquor Control Act ensures that establishments such as bars, restaurants, and retail stores adhere to strict guidelines that promote responsible service and consumption.

The Liquor Corporation Act establishes the NLC and its powers while also reinforcing the importance of social responsibility in the management of alcohol sales. The accompanying Liquor Licensing Regulations detail specific rules that govern the issuance of licenses. These regulations are designed to maintain public safety and promote a responsible drinking culture, which is vital for community wellbeing.

In addition to the liquor legislation, the Smoke-Free Environment Act complements these efforts by protecting individuals from second-hand smoke in various public spaces, including licensed venues. This legislation prohibits smoking and vaping indoors and in certain outdoor areas, thereby fostering a healthier environment for all patrons and employees. Together, these laws reflect Newfoundland and Labrador's commitment to responsible alcohol management and public health, creating a balanced approach that benefits everyone in the community.

## Newfoundland and Labrador Liquor Corporation

The NLC is broken into two divisions:

- Licensing and Administration Unit is responsible for issuing licensing and responding to any licensee inquiries.
- Regulatory Compliance Unit is responsible for conducting inspections, enforcement of rules and regulations by licensees, and education for licensees.

Liquor inspectors are responsible for ensuring compliance by licensees. Inspectors will set appointments for inspections but will also show up unannounced to monitor compliance or to investigate any complaints.

## Licenses

Each liquor license class has its own eligibility requirements and its own license terms and conditions which are outlined in the Liquor Control Act and Liquor Licensing Regulations. While there are over 20 different licenses, here are the ones you will typically come across:

- **Restaurant:** An establishment which is exclusively engaged in the serving of regular meals to the public in consideration of payment.
- **Lounge:** A premises provided with the facilities and equipment prescribed in regulations where, in consideration of payment, alcoholic liquor is served for consumption. A Lounge operates solely for the sale of alcoholic liquor to the public, where food and entertainment may or may not be available.
- **Restaurant/Lounge:** A Restaurant/Lounge License authorizes the licensee to sell to guests at meals, for consumption on the licensed premises, spirits by the glass and wine and beer by the glass or opened bottle during the hours of 9:00 a.m. to 8:30 p.m. It also authorizes the licensee to sell to the public, for consumption on the licensed premises, spirits by the glass and wine and beer by the glass or opened bottle during the hours of 9:00 p.m. to 2:00 a.m. (or 3:00 a.m. depending on the day of the week) with or without food.
- **Delivery Service:** A Delivery Service license authorizes the licensee or an employee of the licensee to deliver alcoholic liquor to persons who purchased the liquor with a meal prepared by a licensed establishment.
- **Hotel:** An establishment where temporary lodging is regularly provided to the public in consideration of payment.
- **Special Events:** A Special Event License may be issued authorizing any charitable or non-profit organization raising funds for charitable purposes that does not hold a subsisting license issued by the board, to sell spirits, beers and wines at a function on the premises and on the date or dates to be prescribed in the license.

## Secondary Licenses

- **Catering License:** A Catering License is a secondary license that authorizes a current licensee to sell for consumption, on any premises during the period prescribed in the license, alcoholic beverages by the glass or opened bottle with a meal supplied by way of an off premises catering service.
- **Patio License:** A Patio License can be obtained if you are a current licensee and wish to add a patio to your licensed premises. The patio or proposed area should be located adjoining the existing licensed establishment.
- **Sampling Events:** if you are interested in holding a Sampling Event that is sponsored by a supplier or sales representative at your licensed establishment, you may apply to NLC for permission.

## Standard Drink Size

Standard Drink Size provides a straightforward and universal method for measuring alcohol content across various beverages, empowering individuals to take control of their drinking habits. By offering a clear benchmark, it enables people to track their intake effectively, understand health guidelines, and moderate consumption, in turn, helping prevent excessive drinking and its associated harms.

This approach simplifies the often complex science of alcohol consumption into easy-to-understand figures, making it a valuable tool for public health campaigns and personal awareness. Whether it's a small glass of wine, a can of beer, or a shot of liquor, recognizing that these options can contain the same amount of alcohol helps make informed choices.

- 12oz beer with a 5% ABV - standard bottle
- 12oz cider or cooler with a 5% ABV
- 5oz Wine at 12% ABV
- 1.5oz Liquor/Spirit at 40% ABV

## BAC Chart

The BAC chart or the Blood Alcohol Concentration Chart is a reference tool that helps estimate a person's blood alcohol concentration level based on the amount of alcohol consumed, their body weight, and the time over which the alcohol was consumed. BAC is a measure of the amount of alcohol in a person's bloodstream, expressed as a percentage.

## Signs of Intoxication

While detecting signs of intoxication can be challenging - given the unique ways it affects individuals - there are several key indicators to observe. By paying close attention to behaviors such as slurred speech, impaired coordination, and unusual mood swings, licensees can make informed decisions. The more of these signs are present, the stronger the case for refraining from serving additional alcohol.

Here are some common signs of intoxication:

- Increased speed or rate of consumption
- Bloodshot, glassy, or watery eyes
- Flushed face
- Droopy Eyelids
- Blank stare of dazed look
- Twitching or body tremors
- Disheveled appearance
- Thick, slurred speech
- Loud, noisy speech
- Changes in speech volume - inability to control volume
- Rambling train of thought
- Unusually fast or slow speech
- Slow response to questions or comments
- Repetitive Statements
- Bravado, boasting
- Making irrational statements
- Complaining about price or strength of drinks
- Foul language
- Argumentative
- Annoying other guests or staff
- Aggressive or belligerent
- Obnoxious or mean
- Inappropriate sexual advances
- Boisterious
- Overly friendly to guests or staff
- Careless with money
- Difficulty making change
- Buying drinks for strangers
- Swaying, staggering, or stumbling
- unable to sit straight
- Restless
- Depressed or sullen
- Crying or moody
- Extreme or sudden change in behaviour
- Overly animated or entertaining
- drowsiness or falling asleep
- Lack of focus or eye contact
- Difficulty standing up
- Unusual walk
- Can't find mouth with glass
- Falling down or falling off chair
- Difficulty lighting cigarettes
- Lighting more than one cigarette
- Clumsy
- Difficulty remembering
- Spilling drinks
- Disoriented
- Agitated, anxious
- Grinding teeth
- Vomiting
- Odor of alcohol or marijuana
- Excessive perspiration
- Repeated trips to the washroom or outside

## Identification

In Newfoundland and Labrador, ensuring responsible alcohol sales is a priority. With a legal drinking age of 19 years old, we diligently check government-issued photo IDs for anyone who appears to be under 25. This practice promotes safe consumption while creating a responsible community atmosphere.

Acceptable forms of ID must be valid and not expired, be the original, and must be examined to verify authenticity. Look for key features such as the photo, description, and security elements.

## Valid Forms of ID

- Provincial Driver's License with a Photo issued in NL or another province
- NL Photo Identification Card
- National Passport
- Canadian Citizenship Card with Photo
- Canadian Armed Forces ID Card
- Secure Indian Status Card
- Permanent Residence Card
- Firearms Possession and Acquisitions License

When checking someone's ID, ensure the following:

- The picture and description match the person using the ID
- The photograph is genuine and hasn't been substituted
- The plastic laminate is intact
- The lettering for the name and date of birth hasn't been altered
- The ID doesn't show erasure marks

Have the guest remove their ID from their wallet and hold the ID in your hand. This will allow you to examine the ID closer and give you the opportunity to feel or see alterations made to the ID that you were unable to detect when it was in the guests wallet.

**Use the acronym FLAG when examining identification:**

- **FEEL:** When evaluating the authenticity of identification cards, it is essential to be vigilant for telltale signs of low-quality manufacturing, including imperfections like bubbles, pin holes, rough edges, and excessive lamination, while also closely examining the ID security features for any discrepancies that may indicate substandard production.
- **LOOK:** When analyzing an individual's identification, it is essential to focus on permanent traits - such as the shape and placement of the eyes, hairline, nose, and lips - while exercising good judgment to verify specific facts, as features like eye and hair color can be altered; also, be attentive to unique characteristics like asymmetrical faces or dimples that may further distinguish the individual.
- **ASK:** In order to effectively verify an individual's identity while ensuring authenticity, it is often beneficial to ask targeted, cryptic questions - such as their postal code, year of high school graduation, or horoscope - particularly when they may have memorized their personal information; additionally, requesting a secondary form of identification can further enhance the confirmation process.
- **GIVE BACK:** If you discover that a guest is using a fake ID, it is your responsibility to deny them service, inform your fellow staff members, and document the incident accordingly; please remember that only police officers in Canada are authorized to confiscate fake IDs.

## Alcohol Combination

Combining alcohol with drugs and other substances can lead to a range of harmful effects that not only jeopardize individual health but also create challenges for those in the hospitality industry. Understanding these interactions is crucial for serving our guests responsibly and ensuring their safety and enjoyment. By educating ourselves about the potential risks, we can encourage safer choices. This enhances the guest experience and also demonstrates our commitment to their health, allowing us to create a welcoming atmosphere where everyone can have a good time responsibly.

## Alcohol and Energy Drinks

Combining alcohol with energy drinks may seem like a fun way to maintain energy during a night out, but there are significant risks involved. The caffeine in energy drinks can mask the sedative effects of alcohol, leading drinkers to feel more alert and thus encouraging them to consume more alcohol than they otherwise might, resulting in increased impairment. This combination can also lead to a host of health issues, such as anxiety, elevated heart rates, and a higher likelihood of alcohol poisoning or caffeine overdose. Individuals who mix these beverages are more prone to engaging in risky behaviors such as impaired driving.

It is Important to note that caffeine does not enhance alcohol metabolism, meaning it won't help sober you up. To prioritize your health and safety, it's wise to avoid mixing alcohol with energy drinks altogether and to enjoy each responsibly!

## Alcohol and Cannabis

The combination of tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and alcohol presents intriguing yet cautionary interactions that can significantly alter the experience for users. While alcohol is known to enhance the body's absorption of THC, leading to feelings of increased euphoria and potential relief from alcohol-induced nausea in smaller doses, the risks of mixing these substances should not be underestimated. Larger doses can result in overwhelming sensations of lethargy, disorientation, and heaviness, potentially making the experience both confusing and uncomfortable.

The unpredictable nature of this combination can intensify or alter the high in unexpected ways, sometimes leading to more serious consequences such as faster alcohol absorption, prolonged intoxication, and even drug-related blackouts. For those considering partaking, it's generally advisable to focus on one substance at a time to ensure a safer and more enjoyable experience, given that while cannabis is relatively safe on its own, mixing it with alcohol demands a more cautious approach.

## Alcohol and Other Drugs

Mixing drugs with alcohol poses significant health risks that deserve our attention and caution. When these substances interact, the effects can be unpredictable and dangerously magnified.

Alcohol can enhance the intoxicating effects of various medications - whether over-the-counter, prescription, or illicit - leading to a host of adverse reactions such as impaired concentration, poor decision-making, and disorientation. This concoction can heighten intoxication levels up to five times, resulting in slurred speech, blurred vision, and slowed reflexes, all of which can compromise safety and well-being.

More alarmingly, the combination can lead to severe health consequences like overdose or even death, particularly with substances such as opiates that can slow or stop breathing. Therefore, it's critical to remain informed and make responsible choices, prioritizing health and safety when it comes to substance use. By being aware of these risks, we empower ourselves to make safer decisions.

## Binge Drinking

Binge drinking is defined as "drinking lots of alcohol in a short amount of time to get drunk"

Binge drinking can be:

- Consuming 5 or more drinks at one time, 12 or more times over the past year
- Consuming 4 or more drinks per occasion for women, or 5 or more drinks per occasion for men within 2 hours
- A drinking pattern that raises BAC to 0.08 or higher

It is associated with numerous health problems, including alcohol poisoning, liver disease, and certain types of cancers and increases the likelihood of unsafe sexual behavior, alcohol-related injuries, and other potentially deadly consequences.

## Monitor Intoxication

Actively offer a wide variety of low and non-alcoholic drinks to your guests. Make sure your guests know these products are available. Advertise them on your menus and tent cards. Promote alternative beverages options by offering them at prices that are competitive with regular alcoholic products. Create interesting "mocktails": exotic names and fresh ingredients will make these products appealing to your guests.

Serving food is one way that you can lower the risk of guests becoming intoxicated by alcohol. Not only does food delay the absorption of alcohol into the blood, but it generally takes guests longer to drink when they are also eating. Offer free or low-priced appetizers for after-work guests: these people may be at especially high risk of becoming intoxicated because they are likely to have an empty stomach. Try a "hungry hour" promotion with two-for-one snacks. Train your servers to market food: offer them incentives for increasing food sales.

## Refusing Service

When a guest is observed to be intoxicated, service must be stopped under any and all circumstances. Approach the situation with a gentle and understanding demeanor. Politely inform the guest and provide the reason for refused service. Do not lose your cool and stand your ground. Avoid being too straight forward and blunt and do not insult the guest by calling them drunk. Use pleasant language with clear communication.

- Do not let the guest persuade you into serving them another drink.
- Do not delay informing the guest in hopes they will leave or not attempt to get another drink.
- Offer to call the guest a taxi or to call a friend or family member of theirs to pick them up. Our guests arriving home safely is a top priority.
- Communicate with all staff about the decision to refuse service to make sure they are not served by another employee.
- Make sure the guest leaves the venue safely.
- Document the refused service in your log book and with an incident report.

## Drinking and Driving

In Canada, drinking and driving is the primary criminal cause of death and injury with roughly 1 in 4 road deaths involving alcohol.

Driving a vehicle while drinking reduces your ability to drive safely. Driving while impaired puts everyone at risk, including passengers, drivers and pedestrians.

Your brain needs to be alert and focused when you drive. Even small amounts of alcohol or drugs can affect your ability to drive safely. Alcohol and drugs act on your brain and body in different ways and can reduce your:

- attention
- judgment
- motor skills
- reaction time
- decision-making skills
- balance and coordination

If someone is drinking and driving, act immediately.

1. Call 911
2. Provide the police with as many details as possible. Try to get the license plate, make and model of the car, car colour, description of driver.
3. Do not put yourself in danger. Observe from a safe distance and allow the police to handle the stop.

- Do not take someone's keys. This puts you in a confrontational position with potential risk to you or the guest. Taking someone's keys is classified as theft under the law.
- Do not put yourself between the person and the vehicle.
- Offer to call a taxi or family/friend of the guest to try and persuade them to take an alternative action.

## Liability

Commercial hosts (bars, restaurants, and other licensed establishments) owe a duty of care to their patrons and the public. This duty requires them to take reasonable steps to prevent patrons from becoming intoxicated to a point where they pose a danger to themselves or others.

The law prohibits licensed establishments from selling alcohol to anyone who is, or appears to be, intoxicated. The provincial Liquor Control Act contains provisions, similar to other Canadian provinces, that specifically allow certain individuals to sue a licensed establishment for damages if an overserved person causes injury, death, or property damage. Family members of an impaired person who dies, and third parties injured by an impaired person, are typical claimants under these provisions.

Courts examine a variety of factors to determine if a bar was negligent and the degree of fault:

- Whether the patron showed visible signs of intoxication.
- Whether the bar had a system for monitoring alcohol consumption.
- Whether staff attempted to find safe transportation for the patron (e.g., calling a taxi).
- Whether the bar knew the patron would be driving after leaving.
- Whether the patron was ejected from the premises while intoxicated.

Beyond civil liability, bars and their employees face other consequences:

- **Fines and Penalties:** The Newfoundland and Labrador Liquor Corporation (NLC), which regulates the sale of alcohol in the province, can impose fines and suspend or revoke a bar's license for violations of the Liquor Control Act, such as serving an intoxicated person.
- **Criminal Charges:** In serious cases involving injury or death, bar owners and employees could potentially face criminal charges, though this is less common than civil liability.
- **Insurance:** Bars mitigate their risk by purchasing specific liquor liability insurance policies, which cover legal fees, settlements, and damages resulting from alcohol-related claims. General commercial liability insurance typically does not cover this exposure.

## Alcohol Leaving the Premises

Once alcohol is bought for consumption at a licensed establishment, it must not leave the premises. If a licensee allows an individual to remove alcoholic beverages from the licensed footprint, it would be considered in breach of the Licensing Regulations.

## Overcrowding

Licensees shall ensure that the number of persons on the premises to which the license applies, including employees of the license holder, does not exceed the capacity of the licensed premises as stated on the capacity card.

## Hours of Sale and Consumption

Alcohol can only be sold and consumed during the prescribed hours in the Regulations. The hours of sale are from 9:00 a.m. until 2:00 a.m. on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, and 9:00 a.m. until 3:00 a.m. on Thursday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday with consumption permitted one half hour after sale.

## Mandatory Exit

A licensee who is authorized to sell alcoholic beverages under a Restaurant/Lounge License or Lounge License must ensure that all patrons vacate the licensed premises by 3:00 a.m. on weekdays and 4:00 a.m. on weekends. Mandatory exit is not applicable to licensees that offer a full service food menu 24 hours a day on a regular basis.

## Minimum Pricing

A standard serving size means: 341 ml of beer; 355 ml of a "ready to drink" beverage; 4 ounces of wine; and 1 ounce of spirits. As the holder of a liquor license, you shall not provide alcoholic beverages to a guest for less than $2.65 per standard serving inclusive of applicable taxes, or in a larger or smaller serving without an increase or decrease in the price in the same proportion as the increase or decrease in the volume of alcoholic liquor.

Responsible alcohol service is a cornerstone of the hospitality industry, and its importance cannot be overstated. By prioritizing practices such as properly checking IDs, recognizing signs of intoxication, and promoting safe drinking habits, establishments can significantly reduce risks associated with over-serving, including drunk driving and violence. embracing responsible alcohol service enhances the overall experience for guests while reinforcing the industry's commitment to public well-being.
