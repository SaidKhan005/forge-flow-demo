# Latin American Words To Know: Ingredients

- **ACHIOTE:** a spice and natural colorant derived from the reddish seeds native to the tropical Americas and provides a mild, earthy, and slightly peppery flavor. It gives vibrant orange-red hue to food and is used as a staple ingredient in many dishes including meats, stews, and rice.
- **ADOBO:** a versatile marinade, sauce, or dry seasoning blend, not only enhances the flavors of meats, poultry, fish, and vegetables but also serves as a preservative, with regional variations that highlight its global appeal - ranging from aromatic dry rubs in the Caribbean to rich red chili pastes in Mexico.
- **AJI AMARILLO:** a vibrant orange-yellow chili pepper native to South America, stands as a cornerstone of Peruvian cuisine, offering a medium-hot heat level complemented by its unique, fruity flavor reminiscent of passion fruit; whether used fresh, dried, or as a blended paste with oils, cream, or cheese, it elevates dishes with its sunny zest and slight sweetness.
- **ALLSPICE:** the dried unripe berry from the Pimenta dioica tree, native to the Caribbean and Central America, offering a unique flavor profile that combines the warmth of clove, cinnamon, and nutmeg with a subtle peppery kick, making it a versatile ingredient in both sweet and savory dishes.
- **ANCHO:** a dried poblano pepper celebrated for its sweet, mild flavor, is a staple in Mexican and Southwestern cuisine, where its rich, dark red or black hue imparts notes of dried fruit, cocoa, and tobacco to sauces, soups, and stews. It is available both whole and in powder form for versatile culinary use.
- **ANISE:** an herbaceous annual plant in the parsley family, known for its small, brown, crescent-shaped seeds that impart a distinctive sweet licorice flavor, making it a popular spice in baking, cooking, and even flavoring alcohols. While often confused with licorice root and star anise, they are all separate plants.
- **ARROZ:** Spanish word for rice.
- **CARAMBOLA:** commonly known as star fruit, a delightful tropical fruit with sweet and tangy flavor, distinctive five-angled shape, and a crunchy texture. Widely grown in Latin America - particularly in Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, and the Caribbean - it is often enjoyed in refreshing juices, salads, and jams.
- **CARAOTAS NEGRAS:** Venezuelan-style black beans that have are creamy and often slightly sweet that are typically soaked and slow-cooked..
- **CARNE:** broadly translates to meat and encompasses all types of edible animal flesh; however, in many Spanish-speaking regions, it is predominantly understood to refer specifically to beef.
- **CASSAVA:** also known as yuca or manioc, is a highly versatile starchy root vegetable that thrives in tropical and subtropical regions, offering a significant energy source due to its dense carbohydrates and remarkable drought tolerance; however, it's essential to peel and thoroughly cook this fibrous tuber to eliminate toxic cyanogenic glycosides. It has a mild, sweet, and slightly nutty flavor, making it an ideal ingredient for a variety of dishes such as flour, cakes, stews, and even tapioca pearls, which can be boiled, mashed, roasted, or fried.
- **CERDO:** a Spanish noun meaning pig commonly used in cooking..
- **CHILE DE ARBOL:** a small, potent Mexican chili pepper known for its bright red color, grassy flavor, and intense, clean heat. It is long, thin, and bright red, and often retains its vibrant color even when dried.
- **CHIPOTLE PEPPER:** a smoke-dried, fully ripened red jalapeño pepper, known for its distinctive deep, smoky, and sweet flavor profile, which develops as the fresh green jalapeños are left to mature on the vine until turning red and are then carefully smoked for several hours, resulting in a medium, manageable heat that enhances a variety of culinary dishes.
- **CHORIZO:** a highly seasoned pork sausage known for its deep red color and bold flavor, that comes in two primary forms: the ready-to-eat Spanish chorizo, which is cured and smoked for a firm texture, and the fresh Mexican chorizo, which is typically removed from its casing and fried to deliver a rich, savory taste.
- **CILANTRO:** a vibrant green herb known for its fresh, citrusy, and slightly peppery flavor. It is a staple in many culinary traditions, enhancing dishes like salsas, tacos, and curries; however, due to a genetic trait affecting taste perception, some individuals experience a soaplike or metallic flavor.
- **CORIANDER:** a versatile herb known for its aromatic properties, features dried seeds that offer a warm, citrusy spice with a unique blend of lemony, floral, and slightly peppery flavors, particularly when toasted and ground, making it a delightful addition to various culinary creations. Note: coriander and cilantro are the same plant, cilantro is the leaves and coriander is the seeds.
- **COTIJA:** often referred to as "Mexican Parmesan," is a firm, dry, and salty cow's milk cheese that enhances dishes with its pungent flavor; while it softens when heated, it retains its crumbly texture, making it an ideal topping for a variety of hot, finished dishes.
- **CUMIN:** a dried seed spice from the parsley family, with a warm, earthy aroma and slightly bitter flavor, and is harvested by hand. It is the oblong-shaped fruit of the plant, showcasing yellow-brown to grey seeds that bear a resemblance to caraway or fennel, with distinctive fine longitudinal ridges.
- **EPAZOTE:** a pungent and aromatic herb native to Central America known for its ability to enhance the flavor of black beans with a distinctive profile that intriguingly blends notes of oregano, anise, mint, and citrus, while sometimes evoking hints of petroleum or camphor.
- **FRIJOLES:** Spanish word for beans. While it refers generally to beans, it often describes seasoned, stewed pinto or black beans.
- **GUAJILLO:** a dried Mexican chili pepper derived from the mirasol variety, is distinguished by its smooth, shiny reddish-brown skin and offers a mildly spicy flavor profile that is both bright and fruity, making it an essential ingredient in pastes, moles, and salsas that boast a rich, deep red color.
- **HABANERO:** a highly pungent chili pepper recognized for its small, lantern-shaped, and brightly colored pods, it has intense heat and a distinctive fruity and floral aroma, making it a popular choice for salsas, hot sauces, and tropical-themed dishes; while the vibrant orange variety is the most common, habaneros also come in red, white, yellow, brown, and green (unripe), offering a versatile ingredient that pairs beautifully with tropical fruits like mango, pineapple, and citrus.
- **HUACATAY:** also known as Peruvian Black Mint, is a distinctive herb native to the Andean mountains of Peru, it has a complex flavor profile that intertwines notes of mint, basil, tarragon, lime, and cilantro.
- **JICAMA:** a crunchy and slightly sweet root vegetable native to Mexico, offers a refreshing addition to salads with its crisp texture that remains intact even when cooked, making it a delightful alternative to potatoes. The brown skin is papery and inedible but the white flesh can be eaten raw or cooked.
- **LOROCO:** a climbing vine native to Central America, particularly in El Salvador and Guatemala, where its edible, pungent green flower buds are used for their distinct earthy, woody, and slightly acidic flavor, utilized in various dishes such as creamy sauces, fresh salads, and as a primary filling for traditional pupusas.
- **MAIZE/EL MAIZ:** Spanish for corn.
- **MANTECA:** a Spanish term for lard.
- **MARACUYA:** the Spanish name for passionfruit.
- **MASA HARINA:** a finely ground flour derived from dried, nixtamalized corn dough, used in traditional Mexican and Latin American dishes like tortillas, tamales, and pupusas. It offers a distinct earthy flavor and has a texture that binds instantly, thanks to its unique preparation process involving soaking corn in calcium hydroxide before grinding it into a fine powder.
- **MASAREPA:** a finely ground, precooked corn flour used predominantly in Colombian and Venezuelan cuisine, provides a soft texture and mild flavor that enhances arepas, and is produced through a meticulous process of cooking, drying, and grinding non-nixtamalized corn kernels.
- **NOPALES:** the edible pads of the prickly pear cactus, offer a unique blend of mild, grassy, and slightly tart flavors reminiscent of green beans or okra. Packed with fiber, antioxidants, and essential vitamins, they can be enjoyed grilled, sautéed, or boiled and are recognized for their potential benefits in managing diabetes, high cholesterol, and obesity.
- **PAPAYA:** a nutrient-dense tropical fruit native to Mexico and South America with soft, sweet, orangecolored flesh and unique black seeds; with a buttery texture and flavor profile reminiscent of both cantaloupe and mango.
- **PEPITAS:** the small, shell-free green seeds harvested from select hulless pumpkin varieties. They have a flat, dark green appearance and a mild, nutty flavor with a hint of sweetness, making them a versatile addition to dishes and can be enjoyed raw, roasted, or salted.
- **PESCADO:** the Spanish term for fish that has been freshly caught and is ready for consumption or sale.,
- **PLANTAIN:** a starchy and low-sugar tropical fruit from the banana family. They are larger and thickerskinned than the common banana and also require cooking - whether boiling, frying, or baking - before consumption. They function similarly to potatoes in their starchy and savory green state and develop a richer, sweeter flavor profile as they ripen from yellow to black.
- **POBLANO PEPPER:** a heart-shaped chili that is a staple in Mexican cuisine, offering a mild heat comparable to or slightly spicier than a jalapeño. It is recognized for its deep green hue, offering a flavor combination of smoky and slightly sweet flavors, particularly when roasted, and transforms into a rich dark red or brown as it reaches full ripeness.
- **POLLO:** the Spanish word for chicken.
- **PULPO:** the Spanish word for octopus.
- **QUESO FRESCO:** a soft and moist Mexican cheese made primarily from cow's milk with a mild, tangy flavor reminiscent of a cross between mozzarella and goat cheese. It is an ideal topping for spicy dishes like tacos, enchiladas, and salads, where it adds a refreshing balance without melting or becoming gooey.
- **QUINOA:** a highly nutritious and gluten-free seed originating from South America, is often treated as a whole grain. It is a complete protein containing all nine essential amino acids, rich in fiber, vitamins, and minerals, making it a staple for vegetarian and vegan diets. Available in varieties like white, red, and black, quinoa has a mild, earthy, and slightly nutty flavor that enhances a wide range of dishes.
- **REQUEIJAO:** a Brazilian creamy cheese spread, with a smooth, mild, and slightly salty flavor profile, making it the perfect addition to toast, crackers, and a variety of savory dishes with its thick, spreadable consistency.
- **ROMA TOMATO:** a medium-sized plum-type tomato with a distinctive elongated shape and meaty flesh, making it the ideal choice for creating flavorful sauces, canning, and salsas, thanks to its low moisture content and thick skin.
- **SAZON:** a seasoning blend in Latin American cuisine that infuses dishes such as rice, beans, and meats with a vibrant red-orange hue and an earthy, umami-packed flavor. Crafted from ingredients like coriander, cumin, garlic powder, oregano, salt, and achiote.
- **SERRANO PEPPER:** a small yet fiery chili with a bright and sharp flavor profile that is 2-5 times hotter than a jalapeño, making it an excellent choice for those seeking a "hot" pepper experience rather than just "medium". With its thick, crisp flesh, this versatile pepper is typically found in green but can also mature to vibrant shades of red, yellow, or orange, and it shines in dishes like pico de gallo, sauces, and ceviche, often serving as a hotter substitute for jalapeños.
- **SHISHITO PEPPER:** a small and slender pepper with a vibrant green hue. Mostly mild with a sweet flavor profile, complemented by a delightful smoky, citrusy undertone. While they are generally gentle in heat, about 10% of these peppers can surprise the palate with unexpected spiciness. They are often utilized in modern Latin-fusion cuisine, even though they are not traditionally found in Latin culinary practices.
- **TALLARINES:** the Spanish term for long, thin pasta noodles such as spaghetti or linguine, most commonly used in Peruvian cuisine.
- **TAMARIND:** a tropical fruit pod with a sticky, brown pulp that boasts a distinct sweet-and-sour, caramellike flavor, has evolved into a versatile culinary ingredient cherished for making refreshing beverages, candies, and sauces. Originally hailing from Africa, it was introduced to the Americas in the 16th century and has since become a staple in Mexican and Central American cuisine, thanks in part to its high concentration of tartaric acid, which contributes to its unique sour-sweet profile.
- **TAPIOCA:** a versatile starch extracted from the storage roots of the cassava plant. It is available in various forms such as white flour, flakes, or pearls and is almost flavorless nature, seamlessly fitting into both sweet and savory dishes. Typically used as thickener for soups, sauces, and pie fillings where it enhances texture with a glossy finish, boasting a higher thickening power than cornstarch.
- **TOMATILLO:** a small and firm bright-green fruit native to Mexico, are often misunderstood due to their name, which means "little tomato" in Spanish; however, they are only distantly related to tomatoes. With their tart, acidic, and slightly earthy flavor profile, tomatillos are frequently described as having a lime-like taste and possess a firmer, less watery flesh than tomatoes, making them a key ingredient in salsa verde. Encased in a distinctive papery husk, tomatillos can be enjoyed in various culinary applications, whether raw, boiled, roasted, or grilled, offering a vibrant and acidic alternative to green tomatoes.
- **TORTILLA:** a thin, round, unleavened flatbread originating from Mesoamerica, serving as a staple in Latin American cuisine. Traditionally made from masa harina, they are now also made from wheat flour.
- **TOSTONES:** twice-fried, savory green plantain slices, popular in Latin American and Caribbean cuisine. They are made by slicing unripe, starchy green plantains, frying them until soft, smashing them flat and frying them again until crispy and golden. Known as patacones in Costa Rica.
- **YERBA MATE:** a traditional South American herbal tea cherished for its earthy flavor and vibrant cultural significance. It acts as a natural stimulant providing a smooth energy boost akin to coffee, while also delivering the nutritional benefits associated with green tea. It's particularly popular in Paraguay, Uruguay, Brazil, and Argentina, where it is traditionally enjoyed through a metal straw.
- **YUCA:** a starchy root vegetable native to South America. SEE CASSAVA.
