## 95 Water Street

Constructed in 1915 and officially opened in the spring of 1916, this striking structure was originally known as The Commercial Cable Company Building. Officially opened in the spring of 1916, it originally served as a vital hub for Newfoundland’s telecommunications. At a time when telegraph lines were the lifeline for long-distance communication, this building stood tall and proud as one of the main telegraph offices in the region. However, as technology advanced and the demand for telegraph services dwindled, the building ceased operations in 1961.

Following its closure, 95 Water Street took on a new chapter in its life when it was sold to John C. Doyle, an American businessman with a vision for developing iron ore resources in western Labrador. Despite his controversial tenure marked by legal battles, Doyle left an indelible mark on Newfoundland's industrial landscape, renaming the building Javelin House during his ownership.he story didn’t end there; the Christian Brothers later acquired the property, transforming it into the Brother T.I. Murphy Centre, a trade school that aimed to help those who had struggled to complete high school. This shift in purpose highlighted the building's enduring legacy in the community.

In 1987, the importance of 95 Water Street was officially recognized when it was designated a registered heritage structure. This two-storey classical revival masterpiece, designed by the esteemed architect William F. Butler, stands out among the remains of downtown St. John’s architecture. Constructed from a mix of concrete, stone, steel, and brick, it boasts fireproof qualities that many of its contemporaries do not share. The building is a stunning example of Classical Revival style, with its flat roof, ornamental eaves, and impressive pilasters giving it a distinguished presence. Notable original features, such as arched windows and intricate decorative stonework, showcase the craftsmanship of the time and offer a window into the past.

Today, 95 Water Street remains a highly distinguishable landmark, one of the few classical buildings still standing in the heart of the city.

## Raymonds Legacy

Raymonds was a culinary gem nestled in the heart of St. John's at 95 Water Street. Operated by the talented duo of chef Jeremy Charles and sommelier Jeremy Bonia, this high-end fine dining restaurant quickly became a beacon for food enthusiasts across Canada. From its opening in 2011, Raymonds was celebrated not only for its exquisite dishes but also for its commitment to elevating Newfoundland cuisine. With locally sourced seafood and wild game as the stars of the menu, every meal was a tribute to the bountiful larder right at their doorstep. It’s no surprise that EnRoute magazine named Raymonds Canada’s Best New Restaurant just a year after its inception, setting the stage for its rapid rise to prominence.

Emphasizing a "farm-to-table" or "sea-to-table" philosophy, Raymonds showcased the best of Atlantic Canada, featuring authentic dishes that highlighted the region’s rich culinary heritage. Diners could savor delicacies like Atlantic cod, scallops, moose, and berries, all masterfully prepared by Chef Charles. His approach to cooking is rooted in pride of place - each dish sophisticated yet honoring the traditional flavors of Newfoundland. The dedication to quality and authenticity earned Raymonds its esteemed spot on "Canada's 100 Best Restaurants" list each year, reaching an impressive number four ranking in 2015.

The restaurant’s impressive reputation extended beyond just food; Bonia's expertise in wine paired perfectly with the culinary creations. Raymonds received the Wine Spectator’s Best Award of Excellence in 2012 - a remarkable feat for a restaurant outside of Canada’s major urban centers. The accolades kept coming, including the title of #1 Restaurant in Canada from Vacay.ca in both 2014 and 2015, and the recognition of Celeste Mah as Canada’s Best Pastry Chef in

2019. Dining at Raymonds was not merely a meal; it was an unforgettable experience, offering a unique blend of rustic Atlantic Canadian flavors and innovative haute cuisine techniques.
In every sense, Raymonds embodied the culinary spirit of Newfoundland and Labrador. With its unwavering commitment to sustainability, local sourcing, and fostering relationships with independent purveyors, it redefined what fine dining could mean in Canada’s easterly city. The restaurant was more than a place to eat; it was a celebration of place, taste, and tradition. For those seeking a culinary adventure, Raymonds stood as a must-visit destination, inviting guests to indulge in a truly exceptional dining experience right in the heart of downtown St. John’s.

## LATIN AMERICA

Latin America is comprised of romance-speaking countries, primarily Spanish and Portuguese, that beautifully intertwine rich cultural and linguistic connections across its diverse landscape. Stretching from Mexico, through Central America, down to South America, and into the islands of the Caribbean, this captivating region is defined more by the deep connections among its people, tied together by a shared history and collective identity than strict geographical borders.

Latin America is often classified as a "developing" or "emerging" region, with Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina standing out as its largest economies, each contributing to the area's dynamic growth and evolution. But before the Europeans arrived in the 15th century, this land was already rich with remarkable civilizations like the Maya, Aztecs, and Incas. These ancient cultures laid the groundwork for the vibrant societies we see today, leaving behind an indelible legacy that continues to shape the region’s identity.

The impact of colonialism by Spain and Portugal was profound, resulting in a unique blend of influences that can be seen in everything from the rhythm of its music to the pages of its literature, the strokes of its artistic traditions, and, of course, the incredible flavors of its cuisine. Today, Latin America is a beautiful mosaic of demographics, reflecting centuries of interactions among Europeans, indigenous peoples, and Africans. This rich confluence has given rise to a vibrant population characterized by a high proportion of mixed-race individuals, who embody the region’s unique and multifaceted heritage.

Steeped in a delightful mix of European, indigenous, and African cultures, Latin America radiates warmth and creativity, inviting everyone to delve into its endless charms. Whether you're drawn in by the lively festivals, the breathtaking landscapes, or the rich history, there's always something new to discover and celebrate in this remarkable part of the world.

## REPORTING TO WORK

### CLOCKING IN/OUT

As part of our commitment to maintaining an efficient and organized workplace, we ask that all staff members be prepared to start their shifts at least 5 minutes before their scheduled start time. This practice ensures that you are in uniform, equipped with all necessary tools, and have your personal belongings stored away prior to clocking in. As part of our commitment to maintaining accurate timekeeping, it is essential that staff members clock out before attending to personal items or preparing to leave for the day. We utilize the Push Operations App for tracking work hours, and it is each employee's responsibility to clock in and out for every shift. If you find yourself in a situation where you've forgotten to clock in or out, please inform your manager or supervisor as soon as possible so that we can make the necessary adjustments. Timely communication is key to ensuring that everyone is accurately compensated for their hours worked. We also encourage staff members to regularly review their hours worked against their timesheets and pay. If you notice any discrepancies or have questions regarding your hours, please do not hesitate to reach out to your manager.

### SCHEDULE

Schedules are created on a weekly basis to meet the demands and needs of the restaurant. The schedule is posted weekly, no later than Thursday evening for the following week, through the Push Operations App.

### AVAILABILITY

We ask all staff members to take an active role in managing their availability through the Push Operations App. It is important to remember that the availability you provide should reflect your other commitments rather than personal shift preferences. This approach helps us create a balanced schedule that meets the operational needs of our team while respecting each individual’s time. Should any staff member fail to update their availability in a timely manner, they will be responsible for finding coverage for any shifts they are scheduled for.

At Barrio Legado, we value the importance of holiday time and understand that our business needs may vary throughout the year. As such, we reserve the right to determine whether we remain open or closed during statutory holidays based on our operational requirements. Should we decide to stay open on a public holiday, employees who are scheduled to work will be expected to fulfill their shifts and will receive premium pay at a rate of 2x their regular hourly wage for the hours they complete.

For employees who do not work on a paid public holiday, employees will receive a regular day's pay, provided they have been employed with us for at least 30 days and have completed their scheduled shifts both prior to and following the holiday. To accurately determine the pay for this day, we calculate the employee's average daily earnings by multiplying their hourly rate by the average number of hours worked each day over the three weeks leading up to the holiday. In situations where an employee works fewer hours on a paid public holiday than they would typically be scheduled for, employees will receive their regular pay for the actual hours worked, in addition to a regular day's pay.

Barrio Legado is committed to fostering a work environment that operates on a foundation of clear policies, procedures, and core values. This approach allows for a measured response to infractions that takes into account the severity of the situation and the employee's previous behavior. The disciplinary actions available include verbal warnings, written warnings, suspension without pay, and, in extreme cases, termination.

The first step in addressing minor infractions is the verbal warning, which serves as an initial corrective measure. In cases where corrective measures fail to yield positive results, termination may be considered as a last resort. This process will involve a thorough meeting with the employee and management to transparently discuss the reasons for termination and the specifics of the separation. Throughout this entire disciplinary framework, each incident is meticulously documented and retained in the employee's file to ensure a clear and comprehensive record of the employee's conduct over time. ### EMPLOYEE RIGHTS

Workers in Newfoundland and Labrador (and across Canada) are granted three rights as part of the internal responsibility system that helps to make sure that everyone at the workplace takes responsibility for their own health and safety, and the health and safety of others.

1. The Right To Know
2. The Right To Participate
3. The Right To Refuse Unsafe Work
The right to know ensures that employees are informed about both existing and potential hazards in the workplace. It also includes their right to understand the measures in place to manage these risks. To support this, employees are provided with education, training, and communication about these hazards, alongside ongoing guidance from their supervisors.

The right to participate ensures that employees are actively engaged in identifying and addressing occupational health and safety issues. This involvement can take various forms, such as reporting hazards, sharing concerns, or offering suggestions for improvement with supervisors, the OHS Committee, or the Workplace Health and Safety Representative. Additionally, joining the OHS Committee or serving as a WHS Representative are also great ways to contribute positively to the safety culture of Barrio Legado.

Employees have the right to decline tasks they perceive as hazardous to their own health and safety, or to that of others in the workplace. It's important to exercise this right responsibly and not to misuse it without valid justification.

## Basic Rights of Workers in Canada

### RIGHT TO REFUSE UNSAFE WORK

Where an employee wishes to use their right to refuse unsafe work, the following three step procedure must be used:

1. Immediately report the precise conditions of the refusal to the supervisor or manager. If the refusal is resolved to the employee’s satisfaction, the employee must return to work. If the refusal remains
unresolved, proceed to the next step.

2. The supervisor or manager reports the unresolved work refusal to the OHS Committee or WHS Representative for investigation. The employer or employee should notify OHS Division of the refusal. If
the refusal is resolved to the employee’s satisfaction, the employee must return to work. If the refusal remains unsolved, proceed to the next step.

3. The employer or employee must immediately report the unresolved work refusal to OHS Division for investigation and resolution. When the report is made orally, it must be followed up in writing within five
days.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/barrio_company_handbook/10.webp)

Where there has been a refusal to work by one employee, that same work cannot be reassigned to another employee unless the other employee has been informed of the refusal and the reason for that refusal.

While a work refusal is under investigation, the employer may reassign the employee to other work that is reasonably equivalent to their normal work. The employee must accept the reassignment until they are able to return to the work first assigned.

While the employee is using their right to refuse, the employer must pay the employee the same wages or salary they would have received if they had continued their usual tasks. If the employee is not reassigned other work, the employer must pay the same wages or salary and grant the same benefits as if the employee had continued to do work. Reassignment of work in this situation is not considered to be discriminatory action.

The employer may not take discriminatory actions against employees by dismissing them, deducting wages, salary or other benefits, or by through other disciplinary actions against them for reasonably refusing work under their rights. Where disciplinary action is taken against workers, that action or dismissal will be considered discriminatory, unless there is evidence to show otherwise.

### OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH AND SAFETY COMMITTEE

An Occupational Health and Safety Committee is a critical component of workplace safety in environments where 20 or more workers are employed. The establishment of this committee is mandated by regulations to ensure the continuous monitoring and enhancement of the health, safety, and welfare of all employees within the workplace. The composition of the committee is designed to facilitate balanced representation, comprising a minimum of two and a maximum of twelve members, with at least half of these members representing the workers, thereby ensuring that the voices of those directly affected by workplace conditions are heard and considered.

The OHS Committee is tasked with a range of responsibilities to identify and address potential hazards within the workplace. This includes conducting regular workplace inspections as required by regulations, where the committee can assess the safety of the environment and identify aspects that may contribute to health risks. The committee engages with various stakeholders providing recommendations aimed at improving safety standards and ensuring compliance with established regulations. The committee also receives and addresses complaints from employees regarding health and safety concerns, creating open dialogue and developing a culture of safety within the workplace.They are to maintain meticulous records of complaints and their resolution to ensure accountability and transparency in addressing health and safety matters.

Regular meetings, held at least once every three months, provide a structured opportunity for committee members to review progress, discuss concerns, and strategize on effective health and safety initiatives. By collaborating with regulatory authorities, such as the assistant deputy minister or designated officers, the OHS Committee actively contributes to a safer working environment, reinforcing the commitment to uphold the health, safety, and welfare of all employees.

### IN CASE OF FIRE

Emergency procedure in case of a fire:

1. Immediately sound the alarm by activating the alarm switch
2. Dial 911 – state your name, the building address, and provide information about the location of the fire.
If you cannot control the fire:

1. Make sure everyone has exited the building
2. Keep all doors closed
3. Leave the building using the nearest exit
4. Walk quickly but do not run.
5. Assist any disabled persons with exiting
6. Do not go back in the building for any reason until you have been advised to do so by the fire department
7. Meet the fire department at the building entrance to provide them with any updated information.
8. Make sure no one is blocking fire department access.
What to do if you hear the fire alarm?

1. Turn off all equipment, make effort to secure valuable and/or confidential information if possible, however,
life safety is the number one priority. Call 911.

2. Make sure doors close behind you – walk quickly but do not run.
3. Stay in single file in halls and on the stairs.
4. Merge alternatively if two different lines meet.
5. Do not go back for any reason.
6. If your original escape route is blocked, proceed to the alternative (pre-planned) route.
7. All physically disabled persons should be assisted in exiting.
8. Leave the building unless instructed otherwise and proceed to move away from the building, at least 100
meters away.

9. Move away from the exterior doorways and continue to move away from the building to allow others to exit
and provide space for the Fire Department to operate.

10. Keep all hallways, stairways, and passageways clear and all doors closed.

### FOOD SAFETY

Food safety is a critical concern in the restaurant industry, as it plays a vital role in protecting guests from illness-causing bacteria and pathogens. These microorganisms can lead to severe health issues, legal repercussions, and even the closure of a business. By implementing rigorous food safety protocols, such as proper hygiene practices, temperature control, and effective sanitization, we can ensure our guests trust our service, enhance our restaurant's reputation, and guarantee compliance with health regulations.

Key practices in food safety include regular cleaning of surfaces, maintaining proper food storage temperatures, thoroughly cooking food to eliminate harmful bacteria, and preventing cross-contamination between raw and ready-to-eat items. Unfortunately, employees in the food service industry may often perform these fundamental tasks without fully understanding their significance. This lack of awareness can cause these essential practices to become mundane, leading to neglect and, ultimately, potential health risks for guests.

Most foodborne illnesses are preventable with the right safety measures in place. By prioritizing practices that prevent cross-contamination and other food-related issues, we can ensure guest safety and protect our operation. In Canada, compliance with food safety regulations is a legal requirement, and neglecting these laws can have serious consequences. Simple errors including inadequate handwashing, improper time and temperature control, and insufficient cleaning, have been linked to some of the country’s most significant foodborne illness outbreaks.

As food allergies become more prevalent, restaurant staff must be vigilant in preventing life-threatening allergic reactions. Ignorance of food safety measures, especially regarding allergens, is not an acceptable excuse for any establishment.

Regular handwashing with soap and water, particularly before handling food and after touching raw meats or using the restroom, is essential. Cleaning cutting boards, utensils, and countertops after contact with raw meat is also vital to avoid cross-contamination. Cooking all meats, poultry, and eggs to the correct internal temperatures, ensuring hot foods remain above 60°C (140°F), and properly chilling leftovers are key practices that every employee should consistently adhere to. By using separate cutting boards for raw meats and fruits or vegetables, and by storing raw meats below ready-to-eat foods in the refrigerator, we can further safeguard our guests' health and well-being.

## WORKPLACE HARASSMENT POLICY

### PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to prevent, identify, and address harassing behavior by establishing clear behavioral expectations, defining prohibited conduct, and outlining mandatory reporting and investigation procedures. It is imperative that all employees recognize that acts of harassment are deemed serious offenses, warranting appropriate action. Individuals who experience workplace harassment are strongly encouraged to seek any assistance they may need to initiate a complaint.

### COMPANY COMMITTMENT

Barrio Legado is dedicated to providing a work environment that is free from harassment. Every employee is entitled to a workplace devoid of harassment, irrespective of race, ancestry, place of origin, color, citizenship, creed, sex, sexual orientation, age, marital status, family status, or disability. Barrio Legado will not tolerate harassment by or against any of its employees in the workplace or at any work related or staff social functions.

### APPLICATION

This policy applies to all employees of the company including, but not limited to, management, ownership, fulltime employees, part-time employees, contract workers, off-site employees, contractors, and interns.

### DEFINITION

Workplace harassment is characterized as any unwelcome or inappropriate conduct or comment, whether verbal, physical, or virtual, directed at an employee that results in offense, humiliation, or harm, thereby contributing to a toxic work environment. This may manifest as a single egregious incident or as a pattern of behavior, encompassing bullying, sexual harassment, and discrimination based on protected characteristics.

Some common examples of workplace harassment include, but are not limited to: Verbal abuse and bullying including yelling, swearing, spreading rumors, name calling, or making demeaning comments about an employee’s appearance or beliefs. Sexual harassment including unwanted physical touch, sexually suggestive remarks, sharing sexually explicit images, or requests for sexual favours in exchange for rewards. Discriminatory harassment including jokes, slurs, or taunts based on race, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, or disability. Psychological harassment such as intentionally isolating an employee, excluding them from work related meetings, or treating them differently. Abuse of authority such as publicly humiliating a subordinate, setting impossible deadlines, or withholding information needed to do the job. Cyber bullying including sending intimidating messages, making threats via email or social media, or by sabotaging someone’s online work reputation. Practical jokes that embarrass or insult someone. Displays of sexually explicit, sexist, racist, or other offensive or derogatory material. Patronizing or condescending behaviour. Vandalism of personal property. Leering or other offensive gesture.

### PROHIBITED CONDUCT

No employee or any other individual affiliated with Barrio Legado shall subject any other person to workplace harassment or be allowed to create conditions that support workplace harassment. ## WHIMIS

WHMIS, or the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System, represents Canada’s national standard for hazard communication concerning hazardous products utilized, stored, or managed within the workplace. This system integrates chemical labeling, Safety Data Sheets (SDSs), and worker training to enhance safety and prevent injuries and illnesses by delivering essential information about hazardous materials. It encompasses standardized labels, comprehensive Safety Data Sheets, and structured employee education programs. WHMIS has aligned with the worldwide hazard communication system known as GHS – the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals.

The main components of WHIMIS are:

1. Employee Training: This is often called your Right to Know. Your employer must provide you with training, instruction, and information about any potentially harmful material you may come into contact with at work
2. Hazard identification and product classification
3. Labels: Workers must be able to immediately see that a hazard is present. Therefore, all chemicals that are controlled under WHMIS and in their original containers must carry a WHMIS warning label that
includes WHMIS warning symbols

3. Safety Data Sheet (SDS): Suppliers must provide detailed information to employers and workers about hazardous WHMIS products used in Canadian workplaces.
### RECOGNIZING HAZARDOUS MATERIALS

Hazardous materials are substances, solid, liquid, or gas, that present significant risks to health, safety, property, or the environment throughout their production, handling, or transportation processes. Suppliers are required to evaluate their products against the criteria outlined in the Hazardous Products Regulations. Should a product satisfy any criteria for a hazard class, it will be classified as a WHMIS-regulated hazardous product. All hazardous products must be appropriately labeled in accordance with the Hazardous Products Regulations, and a corresponding safety data sheet must be provided to the purchaser at the time of sale.

The hazard class and category are detailed in Section 2 (Hazard Identification) of the Safety Data Sheet and are also indicated on the supplier label. Each hazard class or category is required to incorporate the specified pictograms and other labeling elements to convey the associated hazards and the necessary precautionary measures. Employers and employees utilize the information provided on the label and in the Safety Data Sheet to ensure safe practices for the usage, handling, storage, and disposal of the hazardous product.

### WHIMIS HAZARDOUS MATERIAL LABELS

In Canada, WHMIS legislation requires that products used in the workplace that meet the criteria to be classified as hazardous products be labelled. Labels are the first alert to the user about the major hazards associated with that product, and outline the basic precautions or safety steps that should be taken.

There are two main types of WHMIS labels: Supplier labels - The suppliers of industrial chemicals controlled under WHMIS must attach a supplier label on the container before the material is shipped to the workplace. Workplace labels - Workplace labels must be put on any hazardous chemical transferred from its original containers into the new one. A workplace label is required when a hazardous product is produced at the workplace and used in that workplace, a hazardous product is decanted into another container, or a supplier label becomes lost or illegible.

Workplace labels are not required if it is going to be used immediately. If the product is not used right away or if more than one person will be in control of the product, a full workplace label is required.

### SUPPLIER LABEL REQUIREMENTS

Supplier labels must be in both official languages of Canada. They may be bilingual or available as two labels, one each in English and French. The supplier label must include the following information:

1. Product identifier – the brand name, chemical name, common name, generic name or trade name of the hazardous product.
2. Initial supplier identifier – the name, address and telephone number of either the Canadian manufacturer or the Canadian importer*.
3. Pictogram(s) – hazard symbol within a red "square set on one of its points".
4. Signal word – a word used to alert the reader to a potential hazard and to indicate the severity of the hazard.
5. Hazard statement(s) – standardized phrases which describe the nature of the hazard posed by a hazardous product.
6. Precautionary statement(s) – standardized phrases that describe measures to be taken to minimize or prevent adverse effects resulting from exposure to a hazardous product or resulting from improper handling or storage
of a hazardous product.

7. Supplemental label information – some supplemental label information is required based on the classification of the product. For example, the label for a mixture containing ingredients with unknown toxicity in amounts
higher than or equal to 1% must include a statement indicating the percent of the ingredient or ingredients with unknown toxicity. Labels may also include supplementary information about precautionary actions, hazards not yet included in the GHS, physical state, or route of exposure. This information must not contradict or detract from the standardized information.

### WORKPLACE LABEL REQUIREMENTS

In general, a workplace label requires the following information: Product name (matching the SDS product name). Safe handling precautions may include pictograms or other supplier label information. A reference to the SDS

### HAZARD STATEMENT

Hazard statements are brief, standardized sentences that tell you more about the exact hazard of the product. The statements are short, but they describe the most significant hazards of the product. The wording of the hazard statement helps to describe the degree of the hazard. Examples of hazard statements are:

- Extremely flammable gas.
- Contains gas under pressure; may explode if heated.
- Fatal if inhaled.
- Causes eye irritation.
- May cause cancer.
### PRECAUTIONARY STATEMENT

Precautionary statements provide advice on how to minimize or prevent adverse effects resulting from exposure to a hazardous product or from improper storage or handling of a hazardous product. These statements can include instructions about storage, handling, first aid, personal protective equipment, and emergency measures. Like the hazard statements, the wording of precautionary statements is standardized and harmonized. Precautionary statements will be consistent with the degree of the hazard associated with the product.

There are five types of precautionary statements:

- General.
- Prevention.
- Response (including first aid).
- Storage.
- Disposal.

Examples of precautionary statements are:

- Keep the container tightly closed.
- Wear protective gloves/protective clothing/eye protection/face protection.
- If exposed or concerned: Get medical advice/attention.
- Fight fire remotely due to the risk of explosion.
- Protect from sunlight.
### SIGNAL WORD

A signal word is a prompt that alerts you about the degree or level of hazard of the product. There are only two signal words used. "Danger" is used for high-risk hazards, while "Warning" is used for less severe hazards. If a signal word is assigned to a hazard class and category, it must be shown on the label and listed in section 2 (Hazards Identification) of the safety data sheet.

### HAZARD GROUPS

WHMIS applies to two major groups of hazards: physical and health. Each hazard group includes hazard classes that have specific hazardous properties.

- **Health hazards group:** Based on the ability of the product to cause a health effect, such as eye irritation, respiratory sensitization, or carcinogenicity.

### SAFETY DATA SHEET (SDS)

Safety Data Sheets (SDSs) are comprehensive documents that detail the hazards associated with a product and offer guidance on safety precautions. Typically authored by the manufacturer or supplier, SDSs are required to be provided by suppliers of hazardous products at the time of sale. Safety Data Sheets (SDSs) offer comprehensive hazard information regarding products, exceeding the details provided on labels. They serve as a vital resource for workplaces and employees concerning the products utilized. It is essential to consult this information to identify the associated hazards and adhere to the recommended safe handling and emergency procedures to ensure the protection of workers from these hazards. SDSs tell users what the hazards of the product are, how to use the product safely, what to expect if the recommendations are not followed, how to recognize symptoms of exposure, and what to do if emergencies occur.

Inside this border is a symbol that represents the potential hazard. Together, the symbol and the border are referred to as a pictogram. Pictograms are assigned to specific hazard classes or categories.

### HOW CHEMICALS ENTER THE BODY

In order for a chemical to harm a person's health, it must first come into contact with or enter the body, and it must have some biological effect on the body. There are four major routes by which a chemical may enter the body:

- Inhalation (breathing)
- Skin or eye contact
- Swallowing (ingestion or eating)
- Injection (skin penetration)

Breathing in contaminated air is the most common way that workplace chemicals enter the body. Some chemicals, when contacted, can pass through the skin into the bloodstream. The eyes may also be a route of entry. Usually, however, only very small quantities of chemicals in the workplace enter the body through the eyes. Workplace chemicals may be swallowed unintentionally if contaminated hands are not washed before the worker touches food or cigarettes, or if the items are contaminated directly. For this reason, workers should not drink, eat, or smoke in areas where they may be exposed to chemicals.

### HOW CHEMICALS HARM THE BODY

Chemicals pose a risk to human health through various exposure routes, including inhalation, dermal contact, ingestion, and injection. Their effects can vary significantly, leading to issues ranging from immediate irritation to long-term health conditions such as cancer, organ failure, and hormonal disruption. These substances can interfere with biological processes by functioning as toxins, asphyxiants, or endocrine disruptors.

Key Mechanisms of Harm Target Organ Damage: Chemicals can poison specific organs, such as the liver, kidneys, brain, and lungs. Endocrine Disruption: Substances like phthalates and PFAS interfere with natural hormones, causing reproductive issues, diabetes, and developmental delays. Irritation and Corrosion: Acids, bases, and solvents can cause immediate tissue destruction, including skin burns, eye damage, and lung inflammation. Carcinogenesis and Mutagenesis: Chemicals like asbestos and benzene can cause cancer and damage DNA over long-term exposure. Asphyxiation: Some compounds restrict oxygen supply to tissues.

The extent of damage is influenced by the dose, duration of exposure, and the specific toxicity of the chemical involved. Although the liver functions to detoxify these substances, excessive or prolonged exposure may surpass its natural protective capabilities.

## How Chemicals Enter the Body

- **Inhalation:** Chemicals in the air can be breathed in and can cause harm to the body.
- **Skin contact:** Some chemicals injure the skin directly, or can be absorbed into the body through skin.
- **Eye contact:** Contact with some chemicals can cause injury to the eyes.
- **Ingestion:** Chemicals can enter the body by eating or drinking contaminated food, or by touching a chemical or contaminated object and then touching your mouth, nose, or eyes with unwashed hands.
- **Injection:** Sharp objects can puncture the skin and inject chemicals or pathogens into the body.

Chemicals may be harmful to your health. Understand the hazards and take precautions to protect yourself.
