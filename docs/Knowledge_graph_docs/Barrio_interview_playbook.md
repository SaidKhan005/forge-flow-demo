# Barrio Interview Playbook

## Introduction

Interviewing candidates can be an exciting but often stressful experience, with the undeniable pressure of making the right choice. The prospect of welcoming fresh talent into the team brings a powerful energy, as each new hire has the potential to contribute unique perspectives, diverse insights, and a wealth of experiences. They can help reinvigorate our workplace culture and drive innovation, enhancing our collective success.

Yet, with that excitement comes the undeniable pressure of making the right choice. Interviews provide just a snapshot of a candidate's skills and potential, leaving us to sift through their resumes and responses to find the perfect match. To navigate this, we've crafted a thoughtful system of tailored questions that align with our core values and mission, ensuring we look beyond the surface. This approach transforms the interview into a meaningful exchange, allowing both parties to connect on a deeper level.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/barrio_interview_playbook/01.webp)

## Building Our Dream Team

At Barrio Legado, we believe in hiring with intention, recognizing that the real challenges in recruitment often stem from unclear expectations and hasty decisions rather than a shortage of qualified candidates. We're not just looking for someone who's "nice" or has a shiny resume; we want individuals who resonate with our core values, enhance our vibrant culture, and are eager to grow alongside us. It's all about seeking out the right mindsets - those who are curious, resilient, coachable, and adaptable. After all, we're not merely filling positions; we're cultivating a passionate team that will help shape and elevate our brand for years to come!

### The Hiring Flow

| Stage | Action | What You Are Evaluating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resume Review | Review all resumes that have been submitted. | Experience. How often they change jobs. Teamwork activities. Presentation. |
| Pre Screen | Call candidate for a quick chat or send professional email. | Professionalism. Tone. Curiousity. |
| Interview | Conversation driven questions. | Core values. Emotional intelligence. Composure. |
| Optional Second Interview | Position based. | Problem-solving. Initiatives. Team chemistry. |
| Decision | Use red/green flag method. | Long-term value. Mindset. Alignment. Team needs. |

When it comes to evaluating candidates, embracing a variety of contact and interaction methods can make all the difference! By mixing things up, we're able to shine a spotlight on different traits at each stage of the process. This approach not only keeps things interesting but also allows us to tailor our assessments to specific qualities, ensuring we get a well-rounded view of each candidate. It's all about finding that perfect fit, and by focusing on distinct traits throughout our evaluation journey, we can make smarter, more confident hiring decisions.

## Interview Principles

When conducting interviews, it's essential to be thoroughly prepared, as we want this experience to be positive and inviting for candidates, who are also deciding if they want to join our team. We want to show up with a positive mindset and diligently apply our interview principles to evaluate them to the best of our ability!

- Be fully present and actively listen.
- Ask follow up questions to gain deeper insight.
- Do not follow a script. Let it flow naturally.
- Have different people conduct different stages to minimize bias.
- Use same set of questions to best evaluate candidates fairly.
- Encourage genuine conversation.
- Explain expectations for the position clearly.
- Take detailed notes.
- Allow for silences to see the candidate's composure.
- Ask if they have any questions to show their interest and values.

## What To Look For

When evaluating candidates, we must learn to recognize the signals of long-term potential, not just their current skillset.

- Look for curiosity, humility, and their eagerness to grow. Skills can be taught, willingness cannot.
- Look to see if they stay steady or resourceful when under pressure. Change exposes character. Those who handle pressure well tend to be flexible and adaptable.
- How well do they accept feedback? One's ability to take feedback is key for their development, showing their coachability.
- Look for someone with warmth, showing a positive demeanor.

## Green Flags V.S Red Flags

When hiring, recognizing green flags like open communication and respect for boundaries can lead to hiring amazing people, while being aware of red flags like manipulation and dishonesty can help you steer clear of toxic situations. It is important to trust your instincts. If you walk out of an interview feeling unsure about a candidate, it is a no. Trust that gut feeling knowing great candidates do not make you question your instincts.

### Green Flags

- Real, specific stories.
- Ownership over actions.
- Natural pauses and has a calm tone.
- Shows curiosity about the business and team.
- Genuine emotion.
- Shows a pattern of positive behaviour.
- Open to being challenged.
- Appreciates different working styles.
- Has self-regulation strategies and system for multi-tasking.
- Sees opportunities not burdens.

### Red Flags

- Vague or rehearsed answers.
- Places blame or gives excuses.
- Defensive or passive-aggressive.
- Focus on money, perks, or shortcuts.
- Entitlement.
- Makes things and situations personal.
- Views others needs as an annoyance.
- Unrealistic expectations.
- Lacks self-awareness.
- Speaks poorly about others.

### Questions
