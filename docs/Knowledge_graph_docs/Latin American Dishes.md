# Latin American Words To Know: Dishes

- **AGUACHILE:** a vibrant and spicy Mexican raw seafood dish that translates to "chili water." Aguachile differs from ceviche by featuring raw shrimp or fish quickly marinated in a zesty blend of lime juice, chili peppers, cilantro, and cucumber; typically served within 15 to 25 minutes to ensure the seafood remains firm. While the green version is traditional, there are red and black variations, all commonly served alongside tostadas, tortilla chips, or crackers.

![Photo: Pequeño mar, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/01.webp)
- **AJI VERDE:** a vibrant, spicy, creamy Peruvian green sauce that elevates roasted chicken, vegetables, and fries with its bright, herbaceous flavor profile, showcasing the delightful combination of cilantro, garlic, lime, and chili peppers (typically jalapeños or the authentic aji amarillo) often emulsified with mayo and cheese.

![Photo: F Delventhal, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/02.webp)
- **ALFAJORES:** traditional sandwich cookies featuring two soft, crumbly shortbread biscuits - typically made with cornstarch for a delightful melt-in-your-mouth texture - filled with rich dulce de leche and often dusted with powdered sugar, rolled in coconut, or elegantly coated in chocolate. They are considered an iconic comfort food across South America.

![Photo: Aylin Oporto, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/03.webp)
- **AL PASTOR:** a Mexican dish with spit-roasted, thinly sliced pork marinated in a vibrant blend of dried chilies, spices, and achiote, resulting in its signature bright red hue. Traditionally served in tacos topped with fresh diced onion, cilantro, lime juice, salsa, and roasted pineapple, it embodies a culinary fusion inspired by Lebanese immigrants who brought the art of spit-grilling lamb to Mexico.

![Photo: T.Tseng, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/04.webp)
- **AREPAS:** a versatile and gluten-free round cornmeal cake originating from Colombia and Venezuela, that can be grilled, fried, or baked to achieve a crispy exterior and soft interior. While it is commonly eaten plain or as a side dish, it is also the ideal canvas for savory fillings such as avocado, cheese, or meat.

![Photo: Andreji, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/05.webp)
- **ARROZ CON POLLO:** a beloved one-pot meal that translates to "rice with chicken." A warm and comforting dish that varies across Latin America, featuring tender chicken simmered with aromatic spices like saffron and paprika, with vegetables such as peas, onions, and peppers, making it a versatile dish that showcases regional flavors while typically using hearty chicken thighs or legs.

![Photo: Jdvillalobos, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/06.webp)
- **BIRRIA:** a rich and savory Mexican stew traditionally made with goat, beef, or lamb, with its beef variant being the most popular. Its deep, earthy flavor profile and mild spiciness derived from a carefully crafted paste of dried chiles like Guajillo, Ancho, and Arbol are served alongside its consomé, the braising liquid used as a dipping broth that showcases the essence of the meat.

![Photo: B.jars, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/07.webp)
- **BRIGADEIRO:** a traditional Brazilian confectionary, often celebrated for its creamy, chewy texture and intensely chocolatey flavor, made by combining sweetened condensed milk, cocoa powder, and butter into a fudge-like delicacy that is typically rolled into small balls and generously coated in chocolate sprinkles.

![Photo: Rodrigo Senna, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/08.webp)
- **CACHAPAS:** traditional Venezuelan savory-sweet pancakes crafted from fresh ground corn to create a unique, thick batter. They are a popular street food that is typically cooked on a hot griddle, folded over, and filled with soft, salty cheese and butter.

![Photo: Jonathan de Almeida, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/09.webp)
- **CARNITAS:** meaning "little meats" in Spanish; a Mexican dish with heavily marbled pork shoulder made tender through a slow braising process in lard, that is then shredded and either roasted or fried to achieve its signature crispy edges, delivering a contrast of savory, tender meat with a satisfying crunch.

![Photo: ProtoplasmaKid, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/10.webp)
- **CARNE ASADA:** meaning "grilled meat" in Spanish, is a Mexican dish featuring marinated, grilled, and thinly sliced beef - often made from skirt, flap, or flank steak - that boasts a deliciously smoky flavor, thanks to a marinade of lime juice, garlic, and herbs, and is best enjoyed when cooked over high heat to achieve that perfect charred exterior while remaining tender and juicy inside.

![Photo: Sarah Stierch, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/11.webp)
- **CEVICHE:** a celebrated national dish of Peru, ceviche is a refreshing cold delicacy that features fresh, raw fish or shellfish "cooked" in citrus, typically lime juice. This unique preparation method denatures the seafood proteins, resulting in a firm and opaque texture, however, the citrus acts as a curing agent rather than a bactericidal one, meaning the selection of high quality seafood is paramount.
  - **PERUVIAN:** Known for very short marinade times using lime, large cuts of raw white fish, red onion, and chili pepper, with a the milky, intense leftover liquid called leche de tigre. It is accompanied by sweet potato and Andean corn.
  - **MEXICAN:** Finely minced raw fish or shrimp marinated in lime with cilantro, onions, tomatoes, and chiles, often featuring cucumber. It is commonly served on crispy tostadas or with crackers.
  - **ECUADOR:** Features cooked shrimp served in a soup-like bowl with generous citrus juices, often featuring onion, tomato sauce, mustard, or cilantro and typically served with plantain chips.
  - **CHILE:** Typically uses white fish like halibut, Patagonia toothfish, or salmon. It is frequently marinated with lime and grapefruit juice, and is sometimes enhanced with garlic, cilantro, and mint.
  - **COSTA RICA:** Traditionally made with white sea bass, mahi-mahi, or tilapia marinated in lime juice and white vinegar with cilantro and bell peppers. Served with crackers or tortilla chips.

![Photo: Manuel González Olaechea y Franco, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/12.webp)
- **CHICHARRON:** crafted by deep-frying pork belly or pork rinds until they achieve an irresistible crunch, making it a deliciously salty and savory snack or a delectable main dish featuring the perfect balance of crispy skin and tender meat.

![Photo: Russ Bowling, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/13.webp)
- **CHIMICHURRI:** a vibrant uncooked herb sauce hailing from Argentina and Uruguay, combining finely chopped parsley, minced garlic, olive oil, oregano, and red wine vinegar to create a tangy and garlicky flavor profile, making it the perfect topping or marinade for grilled meats, particularly steak.

![Photo: Dominic Lockyer, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/14.webp)
- **CHIVITO:** a massive and hearty sandwich that showcases thinly sliced grilled beef as its star ingredient, while being generously loaded with ham, bacon, melted mozzarella, fresh lettuce, ripe tomato, creamy mayonnaise, and often topped with a fried or hard-boiled egg, all served on a perfectly toasted bun.

![Photo: Ed Kohler, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/15.webp)
- **CHORIPAN:** an Argentinian street food sandwich, with grilled chorizo sausage nestled in crusty bread, typically baguette, often topped with chimichurri sauce or salsa criolla.

![Photo: David, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/16.webp)
- **CHORITOS:** the small black or blue mussels native to the coasts of Chile, Argentina, and Peru.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/17.webp)
- **CHORREADAS:** traditional Costa Rican corn pancakes made from ground fresh corn, flour, eggs, and sugar, that are fried to a golden perfection; typically served warm with sour cream, popularly served for breakfast or afternoon snack, best enjoyed alongside a cup of coffee.

![Photo: Aleat88, CC BY-SA 4.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/18.webp)
- **CHURROS:** fried dough pastries typically piped into ridges with a star-shaped nozzle, that are deep-fried to perfection, resulting in a crispy exterior with a soft, fluffy interior served hot and dusted with cinnamon sugar. Typically served as a snack or breakfast with chocolate sauce or dulce de leche for dipping.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/19.webp)
- **COXINHA:** Brazilian street food with tender shredded chicken combined with creamy cheese in a teardropshaped dough that is battered and deep-fried to achieve a satisfying crunch. A savory snack which is a staple at festive gatherings and a popular choice for a quick treat on the go.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/20.webp)
- **CRUDO:** meaning "raw" in Spanish, is a dish that celebrates the freshness of high-quality ingredients like branzino, tuna, and scallops, showcasing them in a light dressing of premium olive oil, citrus juice, and flaky salt, often enhanced with vibrant herbs, chiles, or fruits for added depth and flavor.
  - Note: Ceviche is a dish of raw seafood "cooked" through marination in high-acid citrus juice while crudo highlights the freshness of raw fish, dressed in oil and acid right before serving.

![Photo: T.Tseng, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/21.webp)
- **DULCE DE LECHE:** a Latin American confection, is created by slowly heating sweetened condensed milk until it turns into a thick, creamy, smooth caramel-like spread, often used for topping desserts or filling cookies, cakes, and ice cream.

![Photo: audinou, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/22.webp)
- **ELOTE:** also known as Mexican street corn, is a beloved snack that features grilled or boiled corn on the cob, generously coated with a blend of mayonnaise, Mexican crema, chili powder, lime juice, and cotija cheese that has become a staple at summer BBQs and a cherished part of Mexican street food culture.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/23.webp)
- **EMPANADAS:** delectable turnovers made from dough and filled with a variety of savory or sweet ingredients, such as ground beef, chicken, cheese, and seafood; these handheld pastries can be deep-fried for a satisfying crunch or baked for a lighter touch, typically shaped into a crescent with decorative edges that not only enhance their visual appeal but also signify the flavor within. While they are a staple throughout Latin America, they vary significantly by region regarding dough, filling, and cooking method.
  - **ARGENTINA:** Made with mostly wheat flour-based dough that is often flaky and buttery and are typically baked, filled with ground or cubed beef, onions, olives, and hard-boiled egg. Regional variations include raisins, potato, or even sweet quince paste.
  - **CHILE:** Made with mostly wheat flour-based dough that is often flaky and buttery and are typically baked. filled with a mixture of ground beef, onions, raisins, black olives, and hard-boiled eggs. They are generally larger than Argentinian ones.
  - **BOLIVIA:** A sweet, baked, soup-filled empanada containing meat, eggs, potatoes, and olives.
  - **ECUADOR:** Airy, cheese-filled, and fried, sprinkled with sugar or empanadas made with green plantain dough.
  - **PERU:** Often filled with chicken or beef, sometimes containing peanuts, and commonly served with lime and cream.
  - **COLUMBIA:** Almost exclusively fried and made with corn flour which provides a crunchy, savory texture. Typically filled with savory ground meat, shredded chicken, or potato and cheese. Usually served with ají.
  - **MEXICO:** Empanadas can be sweet or savory. Northern Mexico uses wheat flour, while southern Mexico uses corn masa. Fillings include mole, cheese, rajas, or sweet fillings like pineapple and cajeta.

![Photo: Horacio Cambeiro, CC BY 4.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/24.webp)
- **ENSALADA:** Spanish word for salad.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/25.webp)
- **ENSALADA CHILENA:** a simple and refreshing traditional Chilean side dish made from sliced tomatoes, thinly sliced white onions, oil, and cilantro, is an essential component of summer meals in Chile, perfectly complementing grilled meats and fried fish.

![Photo: Carlos Varela, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/26.webp)
- **FAJITAS:** a popular Tex-Mex dish consisting of grilled, sliced meat served on a warm flour or corn tortilla with sautéed onions and bell peppers. Often served sizzling on a hot skillet with common toppings including shredded cheese, sour cream, guacamole, and salsa.

![Photo: jeffreyw, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/27.webp)
- **GALLO PINTO:** a traditional dish from Central America; a combination of stir-fried rice and beans, seasoned with vibrant ingredients like onion, bell pepper, cilantro, and the distinctive Salsa Lizano. It is the national dish of Costa Rica and Nicaragua where it is a staple breakfast often accompanied by eggs, plantains, cheese, and warm tortillas.

![Photo: Legendre17, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/28.webp)
- **GORDITAS:** meaning "little fat ones;" a traditional Mexican dish crafted from thick corn masa tortillas that can be fried or grilled. Their plump shape makes them perfect for stuffing with a variety of savory ingredients such as meats, cheese, and beans.

![Photo: Nicholas Lundgaard, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/29.webp)
- **GUACAMOLE:** a traditional Mexican dip with roots in the Aztec Empire, is expertly crafted by mashing ripe avocados with sea salt and lime juice, often enhanced with onions, cilantro, and chilies, resulting in a creamy texture that perfectly complements tortilla chips.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/30.webp)
- **HELADOS:** the Spanish word for ice cream or frozen treats. These dairy or water-based desserts are often enjoyed as cream-based scoops or refreshing, fruit-filled popsicles.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/31.webp)
- **HORCHATA:** a creamy, sweet, and typically non-alcoholic beverage often described as having a cinnamon-rice flavor profile.

![Photo: Sharon Hahn Darlin, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/32.webp)
- **HUEVOS RANCHEROS:** a traditional Mexican breakfast, featuring perfectly fried eggs atop lightly fried corn tortillas, generously smothered in a warm and spicy tomato-chili salsa, making it a hearty brunch option often complemented by refried beans, fresh avocado slices, and crumbled queso fresco.

![Photo: jeffreyw, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/33.webp)
- **LECHE DE TIGRE:** the bright, spicy, and tangy citrus-based marinade used to cure fish in Peruvian ceviche typically made by mixing lime juice, fish trimmings, onions, chilies, garlic, and cilantro.

![Photo: Carlos Varela, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/34.webp)
- **LOMO SALTADO:** a traditional Peruvian stir-fry that embodies the essence of chifa cuisine, combining marinated strips of sirloin or tenderloin with vibrant onions, tomatoes, and aji amarillo peppers, seared at high heat with soy sauce and vinegar, and served alongside crispy French fries and rice, expertly showcasing the culinary fusion created by Chinese immigrants in Peru.

![Photo: HugoMon, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/35.webp)
- **MILANESA:** a comfort food in Latin America, especially Argentina, featuring a thin, breaded, and fried cutlet of beef, chicken, or pork, often served with a squeeze of lemon and accompanied by potatoes, or topped with ham and cheese.

![Photo: Ben Tavener, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/36.webp)
- **MOLE:** a traditional Mexican sauce celebrated for its deep, rich flavor and thick, velvety texture, showcasing a complex blend of dried chiles, spices, nuts, seeds, and occasionally hints of fruit or chocolate, often made with 20 or more ingredients and require hours of preparation.

![Photo: Laurie Avocado, CC BY 2.0, via Flickr](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/37.webp)
- **PEPIAN:** a traditional Guatemalan meat stew with deep indigenous Mayan and Spanish colonial roots, boasting a rich, toasted sauce crafted from pepitas, sesame seeds, tomatoes, tomatillos, and chiles, and is typically enjoyed with chicken, beef, or pork alongside rice and corn tortillas.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/38.webp)
- **PICO DE GALLO:** a vibrant and fresh Mexican condiment that brings together chopped raw tomatoes, white onions, and cilantro, with the heat of serrano or jalapeño peppers, all brightened with a splash of lime juice. Unlike blended salsas, its dry consistency relies on salt to enhance the flavors and draw out moisture, making it a perfect topping for tacos or grilled meats.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/39.webp)
- **POZOLE:** a traditional Mexican soup or stew, is a hearty dish that features hominy, typically accompanied by pork or chicken, and is richly seasoned with chili peppers, garlic, and onions, making it a flavorful meal often garnished with fresh toppings like shredded cabbage, radishes, lime, and avocado.

![Photo: Meutia Chaerani / Indradi Soemardjan, CC BY 2.5](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/40.webp)
- **PROVOLETA:** an Argentine appetizer, features a thick slice of semi-hard provolone cheese that is grilled or baked to perfection, creating a crispy, golden crust while maintaining a deliciously gooey interior, often enhanced with oregano and chili flakes.

![Photo: Horacio Cambeiro, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/41.webp)
- **PUPUSAS:** a traditional Salvadoran dish that features a thick, handmade corn or rice flour flatbread filled with savory ingredients such as creamy quesillo, refried beans, succulent chicharrón, or fresh squash, and is typically enjoyed by hand.

![Photo: Roland Tanglao, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/42.webp)
- **QUESADILLA:** a Mexican dish typically made with a corn or flour tortilla folded over melted cheese, is often enhanced with an assortment of fillings such as meats, spices, and vegetables, and is best enjoyed crispy and accompanied by salsa, guacamole, or sour cream.

![Photo: Ruth Hartnup, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/43.webp)
- **QUESILLO:** a Venezuelan and Latin American dessert that is a deliciously creamy caramel custard with a firmer, airier texture than traditional flan, characterized by its tiny holes that soak up rich caramel syrup. Crafted primarily from sweetened condensed milk, whole milk, eggs, sugar, and vanilla, it occasionally features rum or coconut for an extra touch of flavor.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/44.webp)
- **SALSA:** derived from the Spanish word for "sauce," it is a vibrant and versatile condiment in Mexican cuisine, traditionally crafted from a delightful blend of tomatoes, chili peppers, onions, garlic, and cilantro, making it the perfect accompaniment for chips, tacos, meats, and even stews.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/45.webp)
- **SALSA INGLESA:** the Spanish term for Worcestershire sauce.

![Photo: Dimitri Torterat, CC BY 2.0 FR, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/46.webp)
- **SANCOCHO:** a thick and hearty traditional stew from Latin America and the Caribbean that combines large pieces of meat - such as beef, chicken, or pork - with starchy tubers like yuca, plantains, and potatoes, all simmered together in a rich and flavorful broth.

![Photo: Jdvillalobos, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/47.webp)
- **SOFRITO:** an aromatic cooking base integral to Latin American, Spanish, and Caribbean cuisines, that enriches stews, rice, beans, and meats with its deep flavor, traditionally crafted from a blend of herbs and vegetables such as garlic, onions, peppers, and tomatoes, either pureed into a raw paste or slowly sautéed in olive oil for enhanced depth.

![Photo: Javier Lastras, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/48.webp)
- **SOPA:** the Spanish word for soup.

![Photo: camposart, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/49.webp)
- **SOPES:** traditional Mexican antojito featuring a thick, pinched-edge corn dough base that is fried to a golden perfection, creating a delightful bowl-shaped vessel ready to be topped with refried beans, savory meats, fresh lettuce, onions, crumbled queso fresco, and zesty salsa, resulting in a deliciously crispy exterior and a tender interior.

![Photo: Sarah Stierch, CC BY 4.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/50.webp)
- **TAMALES:** a traditional Mesoamerican dish, showcasing a delightful blend of masa filled with an array of ingredients such as meats, cheeses, fruits, or vegetables, all expertly wrapped in a corn husk or banana leaf and steamed to perfection.

![Photo: Sharon Hahn Darlin, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/51.webp)
- **TAQUITO:** meaning "little taco" in Spanish; a Mexican dish featuring a small tortilla expertly rolled around a savory filling - ranging from beef and chicken to cheese - then deep-fried or baked for a crispy texture.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/52.webp)
- **TEQUENOS:** a Venezuelan appetizer, featuring savory white cheese in a delicate, slightly sweet dough that is deep-fried to golden perfection. An irresistible staple at gatherings and celebrations, often accompanied by dipping sauces such as guasacaca, garlic mayo, or salsa rosada.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/53.webp)
- **TINGA:** a deliciously savory and smoky Mexican dish that showcases tender shredded chicken, sautéed onions, and garlic, all simmered to perfection in a rich tomato-based sauce infused with the bold flavors of chipotle chilis in adobo.

![Photo: Jennifer Woodard Maderazo, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/54.webp)
- **TIRADITO:** a quintessential dish of Nikkei cuisine from Peru showcasing the delicate artistry of raw fish prepared in a sashimi style, drizzled with a vibrant and spicy citrus sauce that enhances its fresh flavors, all served immediately to preserve its tender texture.

![](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/55.webp)
- **TORTA:** a Mexican sandwich crafted with a crusty yet soft roll and filled with a variety of savory meats such as steak, carnitas, or breaded chicken, complemented by layers of beans, creamy avocado, queso fresco, and jalapeños, often served hot and pressed.

![Photo: Hungry Dudes, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/56.webp)
- **TOSTADAS:** a crispy creation made from a fried or toasted corn tortilla, serving as a base for an array of toppings, typically beginning with beans, cheese, or meat, and layered with fresh lettuce, zesty salsa, and creamy avocado.

![Photo: pointnshoot, CC BY 2.0](assets/internal/barrio/training/latin_american_dishes/57.webp)
