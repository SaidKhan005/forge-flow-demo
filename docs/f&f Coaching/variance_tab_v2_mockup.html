FRAME 1 · WHAT HAPPENED

CPLH below OPZ on busy weeks

Repeated 6 of last 12 Fri dinners
covers ↑ carried a CPLH ↓ week

More front-of-house hours were scheduled than the covers needed. Servers had tables to spare, and you paid for hours the volume never used. The kitchen was fine. This is a front-of-house scheduling decision, which is exactly why it is fixable.

FRAME 2 · WHY IT MATTERS

Luck does not repeat

best possible → −$247 loss when CPLH runs soft

This is your most frequent top driver. A hot week hides it. Next normal week the same soft CPLH shows up as a real gap with no covers to bail it out. Worth learning once, not re-fighting every week.

FRAME 3 · WHAT TO DO

Schedule from the math

FOH hours = covers ÷ CPLH target

Before you build next week's FOH schedule, start from the math. That number is the FOH hours the week actually needs, not last week's sheet.

THE PLAYRebuild FOH off the cover forecast and your CPLH target before the roster goes out. Re-check the gap next week.
FRAME 1 · WHAT HELD

Tuesday lunch held its OPZ

In the zone 9 of last 12 Tue lunches
CPLH in zone · service held

FOH hours were matched to the covers that came in. The team moved efficiently, service held, and labor landed below model. That is what a well scheduled shift looks like.

FRAME 2 · WHY IT MATTERS

You cannot repeat what you do not understand

unnamed win → named benchmark

The shift where the schedule matched the volume is the one to copy, but only if you can name why it worked. An accident you cannot reproduce is not a benchmark.

FRAME 3 · WHAT TO PROTECT

Bank the setup

cover count · floor plan · deployment

Write the setup down: cover count, who was on the floor, how the team was deployed. Find which dayparts can sustain it without pushing CPLH above the OPZ ceiling.

THE PLAYSave this shift's deployment as the benchmark and build the same daypart from it next week.
